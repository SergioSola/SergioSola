# Literature Review: Monetary-Fiscal Interactions

## 1. Fiscal Theory of the Price Level (FTPL)

- **Leeper (1991)** — "Equilibria under 'active' and 'passive' monetary and fiscal
  policies." Defines active/passive taxonomy: active monetary + passive fiscal yields
  standard monetarist outcomes; passive monetary + active fiscal yields fiscal
  determination of the price level.

- **Sims (1994)** — "A simple model for study of the determination of the price level
  and the interaction of monetary and fiscal policy." Argues that the government's
  intertemporal budget constraint can determine the price level independently of
  monetary policy.

- **Woodford (2001)** — "Fiscal requirements for price stability." Formalises
  conditions under which fiscal policy must adjust to ensure price stability
  in a monetary regime.

- **Cochrane (2023)** — *The Fiscal Theory of the Price Level.* Comprehensive
  treatment of FTPL, including applications to US fiscal/monetary history and
  implications for policy design.

## 2. Unpleasant Monetarist Arithmetic and Fiscal Dominance

- **Sargent & Wallace (1981)** — "Some unpleasant monetarist arithmetic." When the
  fiscal authority moves first and does not adjust the primary surplus, the monetary
  authority must eventually monetise the debt, leading to higher inflation.

- **Bianchi & Melosi (2019)** — "The dire effects of the lack of monetary and fiscal
  coordination." Regime-switching model where transitions between monetary and fiscal
  dominance generate large welfare costs.

- **Bianchi & Ilut (2017)** — "Monetary/fiscal policy mix and agents' beliefs."
  Learning about the policy regime creates additional macroeconomic volatility.

- **Davig & Leeper (2011)** — "Monetary–fiscal policy interactions and fiscal stimulus."
  Regime-switching between active and passive policies affects the efficacy of fiscal
  stimulus.

## 3. Sovereign Debt Limits and Risk Premia

- **Bi (2012)** — "Sovereign default risk premia, fiscal limits, and fiscal policy."
  Endogenous fiscal limits generate default risk premia that increase nonlinearly
  with debt.

- **Bi, Leeper & Leith (2013)** — "Uncertain fiscal consolidations." Fiscal limits
  interact with political uncertainty to shape sovereign spreads.

- **Ghosh et al. (2013)** — "Fiscal fatigue, fiscal space and debt sustainability
  in advanced economies." Estimates fiscal reaction functions and debt limits for
  advanced economies.

- **Lorenzoni & Werning (2019)** — "Slow moving debt crises." Debt dynamics can
  generate multiple equilibria with self-fulfilling crises.

## 4. Sovereign-Bank Nexus

- **Brunnermeier et al. (2016)** — "The sovereign-bank diabolic loop and ESBies."
  Banks hold sovereign bonds; sovereign stress impairs bank balance sheets,
  reducing credit and further weakening the sovereign — a doom loop.

- **Farhi & Tirole (2018)** — "Deadly embrace: Sovereign and financial balance sheets
  doom loops." Formalises the feedback between sovereign and bank solvency.

- **Gennaioli, Martin & Rossi (2014)** — "Sovereign default, domestic banks, and
  financial institutions." Banks hold domestic sovereign bonds, creating a channel
  from default to banking crises.

- **Gertler & Karadi (2011)** — "A model of unconventional monetary policy."
  Banking frictions in a DSGE framework with central bank intervention.

## 5. Financial Frictions in DSGE Models

- **Bernanke, Gertler & Gilchrist (1999)** — "The financial accelerator in a
  quantitative business cycle framework." Costly state verification with firm
  default generates a financial accelerator.

- **Christiano, Motto & Rostagno (2014)** — "Risk shocks." Augments the BGG
  framework with time-varying idiosyncratic risk (risk shocks) that are a key
  driver of business cycles.

- **Gertler & Kiyotaki (2010)** — "Financial intermediation and credit policy
  in business cycle analysis." Banking frictions with leverage constraints.

## 6. Open Economy and Sovereign Risk

- **Uribe & Schmitt-Grohé (2017)** — *Open Economy Macroeconomics.* Textbook
  treatment of open-economy DSGE with sovereign default, terms of trade, and
  capital flows.

- **Corsetti, Kuester, Meier & Müller (2013)** — "Sovereign risk, fiscal policy,
  and macroeconomic stability." How sovereign risk premia constrain fiscal policy
  and amplify recessions in a monetary union.

- **Schmitt-Grohé & Uribe (2003)** — "Closing small open economy models."
  Discusses debt-elastic interest rate premium and other approaches to induce
  stationarity in open-economy models.

- **García-Cicco, Pancrazi & Uribe (2010)** — "Real business cycles in emerging
  countries?" Emphasises country risk premium shocks in driving business cycles.

## 7. Policy Mix and Welfare

- **Schmitt-Grohé & Uribe (2007)** — "Optimal simple and implementable monetary
  and fiscal rules." Joint optimisation of monetary and fiscal rules in a
  New Keynesian framework.

- **Leeper, Plante & Traum (2010)** — "Dynamics of fiscal financing in the
  United States." Estimates the fiscal financing mix (taxes, spending, transfers,
  monetary accommodation) and its macroeconomic effects.

- **Traum & Yang (2011)** — "Monetary and fiscal policy interactions in the
  post-war U.S." Bayesian estimation of monetary-fiscal interactions with
  regime switching.
