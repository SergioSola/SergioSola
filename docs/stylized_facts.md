# Stylized Facts: Bank–NBFI Interlinkages

Key data points drawn from the FSB Global Monitoring Report (2024), the
ECB-ESRB Joint Report (February 2026), the BIS-BCBS report on banks'
interconnections with NBFIs (d598, July 2025), and BIS Working Paper
No. 1273 (Nenova, Schrimpf & Shin, June 2025).

---

## 1. Scale of the NBFI Sector

| Metric | Value | Source |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Total NBFI financial assets (end-2024) | **$256.8 trillion** | FSB 2025 report |
| NBFI share of total global financial assets | **51.0%** | FSB 2025 |
| NBFI total assets (end-2023) | ~$238 trillion (49.1% of global) | FSB 2024 |
| Narrow measure (bank-like risk activities, end-2024) | **$76.3 trillion** | FSB 2025 |
| Narrow measure growth (2024) | **+12.0%** | FSB 2025 |

| Money market fund assets (end-2024) | **$12.1 trillion** (+15.0%) | FSB 2025 |
| NBFI total assets relative to GDP (2023) | **224% of global GDP** | BIS WP 1273 |
| Bank total assets relative to GDP (2023) | 177% of global GDP | BIS WP 1273 |

**Growth dynamics**: NBFI assets grew 8.5% in 2023 (vs. 3.3% for banks) and
9.4% in 2024 (vs. 4.7% for banks) — roughly 2x faster than banks in every
recent year. The sector has grown ~3.5x in nominal terms since 2004 (~$67T).

## 3. Repo and Securities Lending

- Euro area G-SIB reverse repo lending to NBFIs has **more than doubled since
  January 2021**, driven by lending to non-euro area hedge funds borrowing
  US dollars (ECB-ESRB 2026).
- Dollar repo funding from NBFIs: **~EUR 425 billion** as of Q1 2025.
- Centrally cleared repos: only **41% of euro-denominated** and **4% of
  dollar-denominated** transactions (ECB-ESRB 2026).
- Short-term repo funding as share of OFI total assets: **7.3%**, particularly
  high for structured finance vehicles and broker-dealers (FSB 2024).

## 4. FX Derivatives: The Hidden Plumbing

| Metric | Value | Source |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Outstanding FX swaps, forwards, and currency swaps (end-2024) | **$111 trillion** | BIS WP 1273 |
| Daily OTC FX turnover (April 2025) | **$9.5 trillion** | BIS WP 1273 |
| Non-US banks' off-balance-sheet dollar obligations | **$39 trillion** | BIS WP 1273 |
| "Missing debt" from FX forwards/swaps | **$26 trillion** | BIS WP 1273 |
| Dollar share in FX swap contracts | **~90%** | BIS WP 1273 |
| Contracts with maturity < 1 year | **>75%** | BIS WP 1273 |
| NBFI FX swap segment growth since 2009 | **3x** | BIS WP 1273 |
| Non-US bank dollar funding gap | **$1.2 trillion** | BIS WP 1273 |
| Top 10 banks' share of global FX derivatives | **~60%** | BIS WP 1273 |
| Synthetic leverage ratio (notional/market value, 2022) | **~31x** | BIS WP 1273 |

Key insight: FX swaps "make money fungible across currencies," enabling NBFIs
to invest in cross-border bond portfolios. The $26–39 trillion in off-balance-
sheet dollar obligations is structurally invisible to regulators and prone to
procyclical amplification.

## 5. Risk Transmission Channels

### ECB-ESRB (2026) — Two Principal Channels

1. **NBFI Funding Withdrawal Risk**: A systemic shock triggers redemptions and
   margin calls, causing broad-based decline in NBFI funding to banks. Short
   maturity (60% short-term), homogeneity of providers, and limited
   substitutability amplify the vulnerability.

2. **Leveraged NBFI Exposure / Fire-Sale Amplification**: Bank lending to
   leveraged NBFIs (EUR 432bn) indirectly exposes banks to NBFI trading
   strategies. Price shocks trigger position unwinding and fire sales.

### BIS-BCBS (2025) — Four Stress Scenarios

1. NBFI stress causes credit losses or liquidity pressures for banks
   (cf. Archegos 2021).
2. NBFI failure destabilizes its parent banking group (step-in / intragroup risk).
3. NBFIs stop absorbing risk from banks (SRT market, securitization withdrawal).
4. NBFIs stop funding banks (sudden withdrawal of the 15% NBFI funding share).

### BIS WP 1273 — FX Derivative Channel

- Tighter financial conditions reduce FX derivatives activity procyclically.
- The August 2024 yen carry-trade unwind transmitted to US financial conditions
  through leveraged FX positions.
- Cross-border yen loans to non-banks grew **75%** between 2022 and 2024.
- Maturity mismatch: >75% of FX swaps are <1 year, hedging long-term bonds.

## 7. Procyclicality Evidence

- VaR / margin constraints bind simultaneously during stress across banks and
  NBFIs, creating larger aggregate deleveraging (Adrian & Shin 2010, 2014).
- In the euro area, G-SIBs' role as the primary intermediary means that if they
  pull back during stress, disruptions amplify rather than are absorbed
  (ECB-ESRB 2026).
- US bank credit lines to NBFIs doubled since 2012 → contingent liquidity risk
  through simultaneous drawdown during stress (Federal Reserve, July 2025).
- FX derivatives activity falls procyclically during tightening, reducing
  cross-border intermediation capacity (BIS WP 1273).

---

## 8. Global Financial Cycle Transmission

- FX derivatives are the "connective tissue" of the global financial system,
  enabling $111T in cross-border obligations (BIS WP 1273).
- A steeper US yield curve → greater FX swap usage → larger cross-border
  portfolio flows (Nenova, Schrimpf & Shin 2025).
- Foreign private-sector lenders (mainly NBFIs) now hold more than half of all
  foreign US Treasury holdings, outpacing foreign official holders (BIS WP 1273).
- The share of variance in cross-country asset returns explained by cross-border
  transmission has risen markedly since the GFC (BIS WP 1273).

---

## 9. Data Gaps and Measurement Challenges

- **Private credit**: Severe data limitations (FSB 2024, BCBS 2025).
- **Granular bilateral exposures**: Missing for many jurisdictions (ECB-ESRB 2026).
- **Off-balance-sheet FX obligations**: $26–39T invisible to regulators (BIS WP 1273).
- **Non-EU cross-border exposures**: ~30% of euro area bank securities holdings
  are in USD, pointing to transatlantic linkages not fully captured (ECB-ESRB 2026).
- **Risk data aggregation**: Deficient at major banks, as highlighted by Archegos
  (BCBS d559).
